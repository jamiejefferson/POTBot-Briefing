# The evidence base (condensed)

Why each part of this skill is shaped the way it is. Use this to explain the
process to a skeptical or rushed user, to hold an iron rule under pressure, or
to answer "says who?". Full research with sources lives in the POTBot-Briefing
repository (five domain studies + whitepaper).

## The standard: ownership without authorship

- **Expert witnesses** routinely defend team-built analysis; the standard is
  "can you defend every number", not "did you run every model" — a
  verification duty, not an authorship duty.
- **US FRCP Rule 30(b)(6)** requires one person to testify under hostile
  cross-examination to everything an organisation knows. Courts accept it as
  legitimate — *provided a structured education process happened first* — and
  sanction an unprepared designee "as a witness who never showed up." Standing
  behind others' work is a solved engineering problem, not a pretence.
- **Completed staff work** (US Army, 1942) supplies the exit test: "Would you
  sign this and stake your professional reputation on it being right? If not,
  take it back and work it over."

## Why reading isn't briefing

- **Illusion of explanatory depth** (Rozenblit & Keil 2002): people believe
  they understand until forced to explain; self-rated understanding collapses
  when the explanation runs dry — and stays recalibrated. Polished AI output
  maximises familiarity with zero causal knowledge, the perfect trap.
- **Fluency illusion / desirable difficulties** (Bjork): smooth consumption
  inflates the feeling of knowing while adding nothing durable; effortful
  retrieval, generation, and spacing do the opposite. Caveat: difficulty only
  helps when the learner can succeed at it.
- **Retrieval practice** (Roediger & Karpicke 2006): testing beats re-reading
  ~61% vs ~40% recall at one week — though re-reading *feels* better, which is
  why users resist. This justifies the quiz and the 90-second bet.
- **AI-specific evidence:** trust in AI predicts *less* critical engagement
  (Microsoft/CMU CHI 2025); LLM-assisted writers couldn't quote their own
  essays minutes later (MIT "cognitive debt" study, 2025 — suggestive, small
  n). The same model harms as a ghostwriter and helps as a tutor (Bastani et
  al., PNAS 2025) — which is why this skill operates in tutor mode.

## Why each phase exists

- **Ownership pass (choices + edits):** generation effect (Slamecka & Graf
  1978) — what you produce or choose, you remember and own; what's handed to
  you, you don't. Speechwriters transfer ownership the same way: Obama edited
  nearly all of his 3,477 speeches. Ministers mark up every red-box paper.
- **Pre-brief interrogation:** PDB briefers are pre-briefed by the authoring
  analysts on the *reasoning chain* before the President hears a word — the
  conversation, not the document, is the product. Elaborative interrogation
  ("why?" questions) is a validated moderate-to-strong learning technique
  (Dunlosky et al. 2013).
- **Teach-back gate:** the military backbrief — understanding is proven by
  re-briefing in your own words. Self-explanation effect ≈ g 0.66 (Bisra et
  al. 2018); protégé effect — preparing to teach beats preparing to be tested.
- **Murder board:** used for Supreme Court nominees, PMQs (an aide role-plays
  the Leader of the Opposition weekly), earnings calls (mock Q&A is IR's
  highest-ROI prep activity), and SCOTUS advocacy (Roberts prepped ~1,000
  questions to field ~100, and drilled from shuffled index cards). Payoffs:
  gap-surfacing while wrong is cheap, plus stress inoculation.
- **Premortem** (Klein): prospective hindsight ("it already failed — why?")
  improves failure-cause identification by ~30% and legitimises dissent.
- **Perimeter & exit moves:** deposition rules make "I don't know" a trained,
  credibility-enhancing answer; media training's bridging gives a graceful
  path back to safe ground; the press secretary's tabbed binder proves
  visible notes read as command. Every domain's disasters (ministers caught
  on air, advocates who misstate the record) come from bluffing past the
  boundary, not admitting it.
- **Confidence marks:** intelligence tradecraft (Kent's estimative
  probability; ICD 203) separates known/assessed/unknown and confidence from
  likelihood, preventing the classic secondhand failure — silently upgrading
  "likely" to "certainly" in transmission.
- **Provenance-first:** the appellate killer question is "where is that in the
  record?"; one confident false citation destroys credibility for everything
  else. Hence: verify load-bearing claims before any rehearsal.
- **Day-of warm-up:** spacing + retrieval is the highest-utility combination
  in the learning literature (Dunlosky et al. 2013).

## The one-line case for the whole process

Fluent consumption feels like understanding but isn't; choosing, editing,
explaining, and defending are the only acts that transfer real ownership —
so the briefing must be an interrogating tutor, never a narrator.
